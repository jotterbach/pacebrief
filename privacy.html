Privacy policy

The German version is the binding one.

In short

pacebrief sets no tracking cookies, uses no analytics, advertising or tracking services, loads no external fonts and has no accounts. Your course files, profiles and settings stay in your browser and are never uploaded. Your browser contacts other services only for the map and terrain (when the page opens) and for the weather (when you fetch it or turn on the wind on the map).

1. Hosting and delivery

The pages are served by GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., 88 Colin P. Kelly Jr. Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA) through the network of Cloudflare (Cloudflare, Inc., 101 Townsend St., San Francisco, CA 94107, USA). Every request passes through their servers, which process technical access data such as your IP address, the requested file, your browser type and the time, to deliver the pages and protect them against attacks and abuse. To defend against automated attacks, Cloudflare may set a short-lived cookie (__cf_bm, 30 minutes); it serves security only and is strictly necessary for that purpose (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). These pages load no statistics script. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in providing the pages securely and reliably). Data may be transferred to the USA; GitHub and Cloudflare are certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Details: GitHub's privacy statement, Cloudflare's privacy policy.

2. Your data stays in your browser

A GPX file you open or drop on the map is read in your browser; it is not uploaded anywhere. The demo courses come from these pages themselves.

When you press a save button, the profile, course or settings are stored in your browser's database (IndexedDB, named pacebrief-bike or pacebrief-run). Your choice of colour theme, base map, wind on the map and the language of these two pages are kept in your browser's local storage (keys starting with pacebrief.). Data saved under the planner's earlier names (PaceKern, pacetory, Pacecraft) is copied over once. None of this is sent to us or anyone else. An export is a file your browser saves on your device; an import reads such a file in your browser. Forget everything on this device deletes the saved data, as does clearing your browser's site data. Storing these on your device is strictly necessary for the features you ask for (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG).

3. Map and terrain

When a planner opens, your browser loads the map style, map tiles, label fonts and icons from OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org, openfreemap.org) and the terrain tiles from the AWS Terrain Tiles (s3.amazonaws.com; Amazon Web Services, Inc., 410 Terry Avenue North, Seattle, WA 98109, USA). This transfers technically necessary access data such as your IP address, the map area shown (which, once a course is loaded, is the area around your course) and your browser type. Your course file and saved data are not sent. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in showing the course on a map). Data may be transferred to the USA; Amazon Web Services is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Details: AWS privacy notice.

4. Weather

When you press Get weather, your browser asks Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com, and for older dates archive-api.open-meteo.com; open-meteo.com) for the weather at the course midpoint on your start date. When the wind button on the map is on, it also asks for the wind at a grid of points around the course at the start hour. Open-Meteo receives these coordinates, rounded to about 1 km, and the date, and technically your IP address and browser type; no course file, name or saved data. To ask less often, your browser keeps the answers in its own cache on your device (Cache Storage): forecasts for an hour, past days for a day, recorded weather until you clear the site's data. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in providing the weather you ask for). Details: Open-Meteo terms and privacy.

5. Links

Links to other sites, such as the sources above, only send data to those sites when you click them.

6. Your rights

You have the right to access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and to object to processing based on legitimate interest (Art. 15–21 GDPR). To use them, write to the email address above. You can also complain to a data protection supervisory authority, for example the Berlin Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information. As we store no data about you ourselves, most requests will concern the services named above; the data in your browser you can delete yourself at any time.

4. Wetter

Wenn du Get weather drückst, fragt dein Browser bei Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com, für ältere Daten archive-api.open-meteo.com; open-meteo.com) das Wetter am Streckenmittelpunkt zu deinem Startdatum an. Wenn der Wind-Knopf auf der Karte eingeschaltet ist, fragt er zusätzlich den Wind an einem Raster von Punkten um die Strecke zur Startstunde an. Open-Meteo erhält diese Koordinaten, auf etwa 1 km gerundet, und das Datum sowie technisch deine IP-Adresse und den Browsertyp; keine Streckendatei, keinen Namen und keine gespeicherten Daten. Damit seltener gefragt wird, hält dein Browser die Antworten in seinem eigenen Cache auf deinem Gerät (Cache Storage): Vorhersagen eine Stunde, vergangene Tage einen Tag, gemessenes Wetter, bis du die Websitedaten löschst. Rechtsgrundlage ist Art. 6 Abs. 1 lit. f DSGVO (berechtigtes Interesse, das angefragte Wetter bereitzustellen). Details: Nutzungsbedingungen und Datenschutz von Open-Meteo.
